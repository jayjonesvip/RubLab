BBQ Prep

Should You Use a Binder for Dry Rub?

A binder is a thin coating applied before dry rub to help seasoning stick. It can be useful, but it is optional for most brisket, pork, chicken and rib cooks.

What Does a Binder Do?

A binder gives dry seasoning something damp to cling to before the meat goes on the smoker or grill. It can help create a more even layer of rub and reduce seasoning falling off while you move the meat.

Most binders do not add a strong finished flavor when used lightly. Their main job is adhesion, not changing the recipe.

Common Dry Rub Binders

Common BBQ binders include:

  • Yellow mustard
  • Worcestershire sauce
  • Hot sauce
  • Cooking oil
  • Beef broth

Yellow mustard is popular because it spreads easily and the sharp mustard flavor usually mellows during a long cook. Hot sauce can work well when you want a little extra heat. Oil can help seasonings stick to leaner cuts or vegetables.

Do You Need a Binder on Brisket?

Not always. Brisket naturally develops surface moisture as it sits, especially when the rub contains salt. That moisture is usually enough for black pepper, garlic powder, onion powder and paprika to stick.

A binder can still be helpful if the brisket surface is dry, if you are applying a heavier BBQ-style rub or if you need the rub to stay put while moving the meat.

How Much Binder Should You Use?

Use a very thin layer. The meat should look lightly coated, not covered in sauce.

Too much binder can make the rub thick, patchy or pasty. It may also slow the surface from drying enough to build a good bark.

When Should You Skip a Binder?

Skip the binder when the meat surface is already tacky, when you are dry brining overnight or when you want the cleanest flavor from the rub itself.

For overnight brisket, salt in the rub will usually pull enough moisture to dissolve and hold the seasoning without help.

Final Answer

A binder can help dry rub stick, but it is optional. Use a thin layer if the meat surface is dry or if you want extra rub adhesion. Skip it when the meat is already moist or when the rub is being applied far enough ahead of time to dry brine.
